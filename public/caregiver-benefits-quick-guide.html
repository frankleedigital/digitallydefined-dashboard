Caregiver Benefits & Compensation Quick Guide

You are not supposed to absorb every cost of caring for a loved one. This guide maps the money that may already be available to you — Medicaid, VA, and paid family leave — and gives you the checklists to claim it. No fluff. Just the system.

If you are caring for an aging parent, a spouse, a disabled adult child, or a veteran, you are not alone. Millions of Americans give unpaid care every day while balancing a career, a family, and their own financial future.

The biggest mistake caregivers make: assuming they have to carry every cost themselves. Before you spend another dollar, work the three questions below — one of them may already be paying for part of the care.

The 3 Questions Before You Spend More

  1. Could the care recipient qualify for Medicaid support? Many states let eligible family members be paid for providing care. Check before you pay out of pocket.
  2. Is the care recipient a veteran? Veterans may qualify for caregiver stipends, home-care benefits, and support services most families never hear about.
  3. Do you live in a Paid Family Leave state? Fifteen states (as of 2026) replace some of your wages when you take time off to care for family.

5 · Your First 30 Days

Not sure where to start? Run this. One week at a time, it turns the whole guide into a working plan.

  • Track caregiving hours
  • Gather key documents

Week 2

  • Build your backup caregiver list

Remember

You are not just managing care. You are managing time, money, risk, family coordination, and your own financial future at the same time.

A caregiving plan is not about doing more. It is about building a system that protects the person receiving care and the person giving it.

No more guessing. Find your numbers. Claim what you qualify for. Own your power.

Important: Planning Support, Not Professional Advice

This guide is educational. It is not legal, tax, Medicaid, or financial advice. Benefit rules change and differ by state, program, and individual situation.

Before filing for benefits, transferring assets, or making major financial decisions, confirm eligibility with your state Medicaid office, the VA, and a qualified elder-law attorney or CPA in your jurisdiction.
